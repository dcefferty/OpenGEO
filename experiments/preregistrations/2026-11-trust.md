# OpenGEO Trust-Signals Round Pre-Registration — v1 — 2026-11

Committed before any row is written to a results file for this corpus. Per
`preregistrations/README.md`, the git timestamp on this file is the evidence; a
pre-registration committed after collection starts is not a pre-registration.

## Why this round

Every causal result this project has so far is about **what a page says**. H4 and H9
found that stating the specific answer dominates, and H13 that contradicting another
answering page costs citation. Fact-preserving *presentation* tactics moved nothing in
the Tier 1 scope probe (`results/published/2026-09-20-tier1-scope/REPORT.md`).

The most repeated GEO advice after "add statistics" is about neither. It is about
**signals of trust** placed on an unchanged page:

- **Freshness.** "Show a recent date." Observational industry studies report that most
  content AI engines cite is recent. None separates the date from the content that
  changed with it.
- **Authority.** "Show an expert author" (E-E-A-T bylines and credentials).
- **Tone.** "Drop the marketing language." H4's control was vague *and* lacked figures,
  so tone alone has never been isolated.

Each can be tested here because each is a line of text added to a page that otherwise
stays byte-identical. The round uses the one regime in this project that has already
detected a small fact-free effect: a non-answering target sitting mid-range, where H6
found keyword stuffing at +0.038 [+0.004, +0.075]
(`results/published/2026-08-29-kwstuff-v3/REPORT.md`).

### The conditions

The `control` arm's six documents are byte-identical to competition-v1's `control` arm
(checked: 0 of 288 differ), measured on this panel on 2026-10-25 at pooled CPR 0.565, with
181 of 240 (prompt, engine) cells off the floor and ceiling. Each other arm puts **one line
of exactly seven words** in front of the unchanged control text:

| condition | line put in front of the control text |
|---|---|
| `control` | none |
| `fresh` | `Last reviewed and updated in October 2026.` |
| `stale` | `Last reviewed and updated in October 2019.` |
| `byline` | `Written by <name>, <credential>.` One fictional author per domain, credential matched to it, e.g. `Written by Dr. Amara Nwosu, board-certified physician.` (health), `Written by Victor Lam, CPA, enrolled agent.` (taxes) |
| `promo` | one promotional line per target format, superlatives only, e.g. `Honestly the best, most trusted advice anywhere.` (blog), `The definitive, most authoritative and trusted reference.` (reference) |

All 24 bylines and six promotional lines are in the builder. The authors are invented.
October 2026 is the month the round is collected.

The builder refuses to write the corpus unless every check passes:

| check | rule |
|---|---|
| verbatim | every arm's text after the signal line is the v0.4 control, byte for byte; every non-target equals v0.4's |
| seven words | every signal line is exactly seven words |
| year only | `fresh` and `stale` differ in the year token and nothing else |
| no facts | byline and promo lines contain no digit |
| new tokens | neither year, and no author's surname, appears in any of that prompt's six documents |
| unique | one author per domain, no two sharing a surname |

**Length.** The four signal arms are length-matched to each other exactly. Each is seven
words longer than `control`, which is +9% to +21% on targets of 33 to 78 words. H7 found
that doubling a page's length moved citation by +0.004 [−0.023, +0.031], so seven words is
not expected to matter. H10 compares two arms of identical length that differ by one
token, so it carries no length difference at all.

## Hypotheses

Three primary hypotheses, each with its direction predicted and stated here, so that a
result in the opposite direction is reported as the surprise it would be.

**H10 (primary). A recent date is cited more than an old one.**

> The same page marked "updated October 2026" is cited more often than marked "updated
> October 2019".

Contrast `fresh − stale`.

**H11 (primary). A credentialed author raises citation.**

> The same page with a byline naming a relevant expert is cited more often than with no
> byline.

Contrast `byline − control`.

**H12 (primary). Promotional language lowers citation.**

> The same page opening with self-praise is cited less often than without it.

Contrast `promo − control`.

**Decision rule, for each of the three.** SUPPORTED only if the share-weighted pooled
delta's 95% bootstrap CI excludes zero on the predicted side **and** its Holm-adjusted
permutation p (Holm across H10, H11, H12) is below 0.05. Otherwise FALSIFIED, with the
interval reported. A CI excluding zero on the opposite side is reported as falsified in the
opposite direction.

### Secondary (Holm across the three)

- **H10a — does an old date cost?** `stale − control`. Predicted negative.
- **H10b — does a new date help?** `fresh − control`. **Two-sided; direction not
  predicted.** H10 can hold because staleness is penalised, because freshness is rewarded,
  or both. H10a and H10b say which.
- **H11a — the same seven words, expertise vs self-praise.** `byline − promo`.
  **Two-sided.** Both lines are unverifiable claims a page makes about itself, matched in
  length and position; this asks whether engines treat a claim of expertise differently
  from a claim of quality.

## Analysis plan

Executed by `analyze_trust.py --runs results/runs_trust.jsonl`.

- **Primary metric:** target CPR (`METHODOLOGY.md` A1).
- **Pairing:** per (prompt, model). Deltas are taken within each cell, weighted across
  models within a prompt, and prompts are the resampling unit.
- **Inference:** bootstrap over prompts, 10,000 resamples, 95% percentile CIs; sign-flip
  permutation test on the per-prompt deltas, 20,000 permutations. No distributional
  assumptions; no scipy.
- **Pooling:** share-weighted by `engine_weights.py` for the decision (ChatGPT 0.559,
  Gemini 0.284, Claude 0.094, DeepSeek 0.037, Grok 0.026), with the unweighted pool printed
  beside it. **Per-engine results are primary**, with Holm across the five engines for each
  hypothesis.
- **Boundary rule:** an engine whose pooled `control` CPR is within 0.02 of 0 or 1 reports
  its deltas as bounds, not estimates (§9). From competition-v1 no engine is expected to be
  there: its five engines' control CPR ran 0.397 to 0.659.
- **Model exclusion:** standing rule. Any model above 10% no-cite is excluded from pooled
  figures and reported separately.
- **No prompt exclusions.**

### Echo, read mechanically (descriptive)

In each signal arm the analyser reports the share of answers that repeat the signal: the
year (`2026` or `2019`), the author's surname, or one of the promotional markers listed in
the corpus. No model judge. The builder guarantees that none of these tokens is in any
document of the `control` arm, so the `control` column is the reader's false-positive
rate. Echo is reported, not tested. It answers a buyer's second question: whether the
engine passes the signal on to the reader ("according to a 2019 guide…").

### Verified before collection

**`make_mock_trust.py` plants known effects for this design.** On 28,800 mock runs (seed
42) the analyser recovers:

| contrast | planted | recovered, share-weighted |
|---|---|---|
| H10 | +0.118 | +0.100 [+0.074, +0.126] |
| H11 | +0.055 | +0.029 [+0.002, +0.055] |
| H12 | −0.064 | −0.081 [−0.106, −0.056] |
| H10a | −0.074 | −0.085 [−0.110, −0.061] |
| H10b | +0.044 | +0.015 [−0.008, +0.037] |
| H11a | +0.119 | +0.110 [+0.085, +0.135] |

On this seed two of six intervals miss their planted value narrowly, H11 and H10b, both
because the mock's shared `control` arm drew about 0.02 high. Over 20 seeds, **116 of 120
intervals contain the planted value (96.7%)** against a nominal 95%. The echo reader
recovered every planted echo rate within 0.003, with 0.000 false positives in `control`.

**Under a planted null**, 300 mock rounds (100 at 12 runs per cell, 200 at 24) gave a false
SUPPORTED verdict for H10 in 1.3%, H11 in 0.7% and H12 in 0.7% of rounds, under the
one-sided nominal 2.5%. Two-sided CIs excluded zero in 5.7%, 5.7% and 6.0%.

### Power, stated honestly

The mock's share-weighted intervals are about ±0.026 wide, so the round can detect a
pooled effect of roughly **3 points or more**. H6's +0.038 would be detected; an effect
of 1 to 2 points would come back as an interval that includes zero, and it will be
reported as that interval, not as "no effect". The share-weighted pool is 56% ChatGPT, so a
pooled result can be carried by one engine, as H13's was. Per-engine results are reported
for that reason, and each engine alone is less powered than the pool.

## Stopping rule

One full round. No interim analysis, no adding models, prompts or arms after seeing
results. A round that fails on data health is rerun in full, and the failed attempt is
archived, not merged.

## Reporting

Published under `results/published/` whatever the outcome. The headline is the three
primary deltas side by side, each with both arm means, its interval and its per-engine
breakdown, because the advice under test is usually given as a bundle and a reader needs to
see which part, if any, does anything. A null on all three is the finding that matters most
to a buyer and is reported as prominently as a positive one.

Every sentence carries the Tier 1 caveat: synthesis stage only, retrieval held constant.

## Limitations known in advance

- **Retrieval is held constant.** Freshness and authority may matter most at the retrieval
  and ranking stage, which this design removes by construction. A null here says the
  synthesis stage ignores the signal, not that a search index does.
- **The engines are not told today's date.** The harness system prompt carries no date, so
  "October 2026" is judged against each model's own sense of the present, which may predate
  it. That is how the API plane is queried, but it means H10 measures relative age, not
  distance from a known today.
- **One wording per signal.** A different date format, a byline with a photo or link, or a
  stronger promotional register could behave differently. The round measures these lines,
  not the category.
- **The signal is in the first line.** Position within the page is not varied.
- **The bylines are fictional and unverifiable.** So are most real ones to an engine
  reading text only. Domain-matched credentials are deliberate; a mismatched credential is
  not tested.
- **The documents are authored**, like every v0.4-based round.
- **Format is nested within prompt.**
